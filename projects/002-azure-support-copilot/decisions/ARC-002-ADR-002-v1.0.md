# ADR-002: Confidence-threshold handoff to human agents instead of full AI autonomy

## Context

The copilot could, in principle, send answers directly to customers without agent review once confidence is high enough. Architecture principle P1 explicitly rules out full autonomy for v1, and business requirement BR-003 makes agents the accountable owner of every ticket. The open design question was *how* the human stays in the loop: review every response, review none, or review based on a measured confidence score.

## Decision

Every AI-generated draft is scored for confidence at generation time. Drafts **below** the threshold are routed to the human queue with the draft shown only as a hint. Drafts **at or above** the threshold are still shown to the agent for one-click approve/edit/reject during the pilot and initial GA phases (FR-004) — direct-to-customer send without agent review is explicitly deferred to a future phase requiring its own business case and DPIA update.

## Alternatives Considered

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Review every response, no threshold | Simplest to reason about; maximum human oversight | Doesn't reduce agent effort on high-confidence, low-risk answers — undermines BR-001 (reduce first-response time) |
| Send high-confidence answers directly to the customer, no agent review | Maximum efficiency gain | Violates P1 and BR-003 at current trust/evaluation maturity; no production evidence yet that the confidence score reliably predicts correctness for this KB |
| Confidence-threshold handoff, agent always in loop (chosen) | Balances efficiency gain (agent reviews a good draft instead of writing from scratch) against risk (agent always has final say); threshold is tunable as evaluation data accumulates | Requires a well-calibrated confidence score, and doesn't yet realise the largest efficiency gain (autonomous send) available from the platform |

## Consequences

**Positive:**
- Every customer-facing response has a human accountable for it, satisfying BR-003 and P1.
- The confidence threshold gives the team a single, tunable dial to widen or narrow AI involvement as evaluation data (NFR-005) accumulates, without a redesign.
- Creates a natural, auditable dataset (approve/edit/reject decisions) that feeds future evaluation and, eventually, the business case for direct-to-customer send.

**Negative:**
- Does not yet deliver the largest possible efficiency gain, since even high-confidence answers require agent time to review.
- Confidence-score calibration is itself a non-trivial ongoing task — a miscalibrated threshold either overloads agents with reviews or lets weak answers through as "high confidence".

**Follow-up:** Direct-to-customer send for the highest-confidence band is the natural next architectural decision once pilot data shows the confidence score is well-calibrated (see ARC-002-STRAT-v1.0 roadmap) — it will need its own ADR, updated DPIA, and Legal sign-off before being considered.
